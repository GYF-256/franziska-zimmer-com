About me

I am a researcher in user behavior and human-computer interaction, specializing in large-scale data analysis, log data, and survey-based methods to understand how users interact with digital systems.

Researcher in data-driven information science & digital systems

My work focuses on analyzing behavioral patterns using interaction data, surveys, and computational methods. I study how user behavior can be measured and analyzed at scale, and how behavioral signals can be used to identify patterns across users and understand differences in how people interact with digital systems.

My research spans three main areas:

  • Large-scale behavioral data analysis, including log data, spatiotemporal data, and pattern analysis
  • Quantitative and mixed-methods user research, including surveys, interviews, and behavioral measurement
  • Analysis of online behavior and user-generated content, including forums, social media, and communication patterns

To support this work, I apply a range of quantitative and computational approaches, including log data analysis, statistical modeling, machine learning for classification and pattern detection, survey design and analysis, and large-scale content analysis.

My work includes analyzing datasets ranging from hundreds of users to tens of millions of records to uncover behavioral patterns in real-world systems, with the goal of generating insights that help better understand user behavior in digital environments.

Currently, I am a Project Assistant Professor at the Graduate School of Information Science and Technology at The University of Tokyo, where I am affiliated with the Social ICT Research Center and Yamaguchi Laboratory. My research focuses on behavioral biometrics and user behavior analysis in large-scale digital systems.

Previously, I conducted research and teaching at Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, Germany, where I studied user behavior on social media, misinformation, and information interaction in online environments.

This work formed the basis of my PhD research under the supervision of Prof. Wolfgang G. Stock.
